Wet Carpet Drying: What Coon Rapids Homeowners Should Know

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. Up top the fibers may seem barely damp, yet the pad has filled up the way a sponge does and the subfloor under it has gotten wet as well. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. In Coon Rapids, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

Warning Signs

  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet
  • A rust colored line at the wall edge, a sign of wet tack strip
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall
  • Loose, wavy sections where the tack strip no longer holds the carpet

Our Process

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Find the Edges

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry Every Layer

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reset

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

After a week

Past Saving

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Furniture Marks

Furniture legs sitting on wet carpet release wood stain and rust into the fibers. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

Wet Wall Bottoms

Under the carpet, water travels until it hits the bottom of the walls. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.

Mold Under the Carpet

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.

Water Held in the Pad

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. Long after the top feels dry, that pad is still passing moisture up into the carpet and down into the floor.
