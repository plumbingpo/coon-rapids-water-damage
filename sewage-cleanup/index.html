Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across Coon Rapids, Coon Rapids Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Signs You Need Sewage Cleanup in Coon Rapids

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

Sewage Cleanup: What Homeowners Ask

The emergency phase, meaning sewage removal plus decontamination, generally lasts 1 to 3 days. Complete restoration, including material replacement, may take 1 to 3 weeks depending on the extent of damage. Throughout, the focus is on returning your home to a safe, livable state without delay.
In Coon Rapids, a sewage cleanup usually runs $2,000 to $10,000 or more, and the total reflects how large the affected area is and how far contamination spread. Expect roughly $1,500 to $3,000 when the backup is minor and contained, and more than $15,000 in cases of extensive flooding that need materials replaced and rebuilding done.
Nobody, pets included, should walk through the area the sewage reached. Stop using sinks, toilets and appliances that drain, since more water makes the backup worse. If the panel and thermostat can be reached without contact with sewage, shut off electricity to that area and stop the air system. Leave the cleanup alone, and wash well with soap if any got on your skin.
Some things can be kept and others cannot. Hard, non-porous items such as metal, glass, sealed tile and solid plastic can usually be cleaned and disinfected. Upholstery, mattresses, carpet with its pad, insulation and drywall soak up sewage and cannot be made safe, so they are thrown out. Each discarded item is documented, giving you a record to use with your insurer.
In most cases our advice is to relocate temporarily while the cleanup happens, above all when contamination is widespread. There are health risks from pathogens that become airborne and from the products we use to decontaminate. We will advise you based on the specific situation in your home.
