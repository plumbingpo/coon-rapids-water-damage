How to Waterproof Your Basement in Coon Rapids, MN

Home / Blog / Basement Water Prevention Tips in Coon Rapids, MN

A dry basement protects your home's structure, the air you breathe indoors, and what the property is worth. Even so, plenty of Coon Rapids, MN houses have wet basements, with problems that run from a little seepage up to a full flood. Below, Coon Rapids Restoration explains why water gets into basements and which solutions reliably keep a Coon Rapids basement dry.

How Water Gets Into a Basement

Water gets into basements in several ways, and knowing the source is key to picking the right fix. Soaked soil presses on the foundation, and that hydrostatic pressure drives moisture through any crack, joint, or pore in the concrete. Clay soils swell when wet, and that sideways pressure can open cracks in a foundation wall where water then enters. Surface water from poor grading, clogged gutters, or weak drainage runs against your foundation and finds a way in. A basement can also be damp without any leak at all, because humid warm air condenses on cool walls and floors.

Exterior Waterproofing Solutions

Long term, nothing works as well as handling the water outside, before it reaches the wall. The work involves digging out around the foundation, coating the walls with a waterproof membrane, and adding or improving the drain tile system outside. It is the most expensive approach, but it addresses the problem at its source and gives the most complete protection. Proper grading, with soil sloping away from your foundation at least 1 inch per foot for the first 6 feet, is one of the most cost-effective exterior improvements.

Interior Waterproofing Solutions

Interior waterproofing systems manage water that gets through the foundation instead of keeping it from reaching the foundation. Interior French drain systems collect water along the perimeter of the basement and send it to a sump pump for discharge. Sump pump systems, ideally with battery backup, keep water under control during heavy rain and power outages. Sealants and waterproof coatings applied inside help with light seepage and efflorescence, yet they are no substitute for real drainage if a lot of water is coming in.

Sump Pump Care and Testing

If your Coon Rapids home has a sump pump, keep up with its maintenance. Test your sump pump quarterly by pouring water into the pit until the pump turns on. Pay attention to any strange noise, since it may mean a part is failing. Basements flood most often when the power is out, so add a battery backup system. For more protection still, think about a second, water-driven pump. Make sure the discharge line sends water at least 6 feet from your foundation and has a check valve to prevent backflow.
